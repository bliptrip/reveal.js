## Framing decisions

**The role.** In the pre-interview call Whitworth said phenotyping/phenomics is your focus but the position you are being considered for is the **Molecular Geneticist** slot (the vacancy left by Noelle Anglin's retirement); genotyping/sequencing is more likely outsourced than in-house; the team is down to Whitworth and one technician, with the two scientist hires first and technicians later. The vision is therefore built on the Molecular Geneticist's written responsibilities — "develop and deploy molecular markers," "interpret DNA-based results to guide breeding decisions," "develop, store and manage populations for trait discovery" — with phenomics as the thing that makes those markers worth having. Everything proposed is automatable and runs on outsourced genotyping.

**The cycle slide (A3).** Same honest framing as the cacao version: the recurrent cycle (parent → parent) shortens with genomic prediction; the release pipeline (the "12–15 year timelines" in SGPG's own FY2025 report) mostly keeps its calendar years; what changes is time-to-decision. Levers placed where they act: (1) markers on seedling tubers before the first field year (FY2025: 80% of 144 families have a *Ry* parent; FY2024: 41 of 163 year-2 entries *Ry*-confirmed vs 7.5% in 2020); (2) measurement in the early, single-hill generations where selection intensity is highest and phenotyping worst; (3) Tri-State / Western Regional multi-site trials stay — G×E is real (Agha et al. 2024, National Chip Processing Trial) — and prediction trains on them; (4) a green loop back to parent choice. Phase lengths illustrative; invite Whitworth to correct. Figure: `static/images/cycle/cycle_potato.svg`, editable.

**The three aims.** Aim 1 markers before the field (DArTag as one outsourced assay carrying the trait markers plus a genome-wide backbone; dosage-aware parent choice; GS for polygenic traits on Tri-State data). Aim 2 phenotypes that are numbers (canopy emergence/closure/senescence as LMI-style indices, maturity-corrected for *StCDF1*; canopy response under contrasting N and irrigation for Objective 1.C; cut-tuber imaging of PMTV necrosis with Whitworth, plugged into Feldman's tuber workflow). Aim 3 populations that do double duty (harmonize A08241, A15001, *S. tuberosum* × *S. microdontum* and UI's Eden × Western Russet on one marker set and DM v6.1 as a year-one paper; design new PCN/PMTV/wild-donor crosses as mappable F1 families from day one).

**The diplomacy that matters most: Max Feldman (ARS Prosser/Wapato).** His group already does tuber machine vision (validated on Aberdeen's own A08241 population; 2,000+ lines/yr), weekly multispectral drone flights, a greenhouse PMTV assay and a wild-species PMTV screen. The deck says, repeatedly, "plug in, don't rebuild": the Aberdeen addition is canopy genetics, the populations, the MAS pipeline and the pathology link — one pipeline across two ARS locations. He may be on Teams.

**Honesty lines to volunteer.** Not a pathologist; not a potato breeder yet; cranberry is diploid (2n = 2x = 24), potato autotetraploid (2n = 4x = 48) — same x = 12, the design transfers, the dosage layer is potato's; no hands-on polyploid genetics — cranberry is diploid; CJ has applied some Endelman-group (and other) polyploid-ready tools to diploid cranberry data, but never to polyploid-specific problems (dosage calling, double reduction, tetrasomic linkage), and the deck must not imply otherwise (⚠ PLOIDY notes on `background`, `ch2-workflow`, `vision-headline`, `vision-aim1`, `questions`, `backup-not-claiming`, which also gained a "Not yet a polyploid geneticist" bullet); maturity confounds canopy-based "resistance" and "efficiency" unless corrected.

## Stakeholders and research landscape (pulled 2026-09-22)

### SGPG, Aberdeen — project 2050-21000-036-000-D, *Genetic Improvement of Potato for Sustainable Production and Enhanced Tuber Qualities for the Western United States* (Mar 2023 – Feb 2028)

- **Objectives:** (1) release varieties with improved processing, pest/pathogen resistance, nutrient and water use efficiency, stress resilience (1.A processing/fresh-market, 1.B pest and pathogen resistance, 1.C nutrient/water efficiency and stress resilience); (2) discover and incorporate resistance genes from wild and domestic potato; (3) develop and deploy molecular markers; (4) research PMTV and potato cyst nematodes and incorporate resistance. Approach: modified backcross, MAS, RCB field trials, greenhouse and lab work. Team as written: breeder, molecular geneticist, pathologist + five technicians.
- **People:** Jonathan Whitworth (Research Plant Pathologist, hiring manager); Kathy Esvelt Klos (acting Research Leader; small-grains geneticist); Rich Novy (breeder; 37 varieties; retired) and Noelle Anglin (molecular geneticist; retired) — the two open positions; technical staff on the schedule: Darren Hall, Karolyn Allred, Demetrius Ragland; admin: Tyler Williams (AO), Jessica Lopez.
- **Impact line:** varieties from this program = **21% of U.S. seed potato acreage** (position announcement). Program history: USDA breeding in Aberdeen since 1949; Northwest (Tri-State) Potato Variety Development Program since 1983; 40+ varieties.
- **FY2024:** Becca Rose and Rainier Russet received PVP and were licensed (Rainier: Russet Burbank-level yield, better marketability, high protein, common scab and dry rot resistance; 8.5 seed acres → 85 commercial acres). A12305-2adg and AFA5661-8 pending release, both *Ry<sub>adg</sub>*. MAS on year-2 material: 47 of 163 kept (28.8%), 41 *Ry*-confirmed (vs 14, 7.5%, in 2020); new multiplex PCR assay. GWAS: TRV QTL on chr 9, multiple PMTV QTL. 35 wild genomes sequenced (23 compared). *S. tuberosum* × *S. microdontum* population genotyped and phenotyped for greening. PCN: selections carrying *GpaIV* (Eden) or *Gpa5* (Maria Huanca) sent to UI for *G. pallida* testing (earlier scores 7–8 on 1–9). PMTV: seven russets screened, Castle Russet some resistance and now a parent.
- **FY2025:** seedling tubers from 144 families; 80% with ≥ 1 *Ry* parent. A12305-2adg and AFA5661-8 in PVP data collection. *S. microdontum* population (220) DArT-sequenced + long reads on parents, grandparents and 13 accessions → greening (chr 2), carotenoids (chr 5), TGA, antioxidants, protein, vitamin C; soft-rot resistance segregating. Marker testing extended to third-year material: PVY, TRV, PLRV, PCN. PMTV: four years, two Idaho sites — all varieties susceptible, asymptomatic infections common except Castle Russet; virus evenly distributed in tubers at high infection; tool decontamination shows cross-contamination false positives. **A16261-2PCN** reached advanced trials — *G. pallida* 5, *G. ellingtonae* 8 (1–9); 4–5 years from release; first PCN-resistant line at that stage.

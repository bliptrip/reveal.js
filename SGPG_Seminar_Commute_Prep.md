### What is a "*Ry* parent"?
- *Ry* = a gene for extreme (near-immune) resistance to potato virus Y (PVY). Main sources: *Ry<sub>adg</sub>* (*S. andigena*; the "adg" in A12305-2adg), *Ry<sub>sto</sub>* (*S. stoloniferum*), *Ry<sub>chc</sub>* (*S. chacoense*).
- "80% of 144 families have a *Ry* parent" (FY2025): 80% of this year's seedling families came from crosses where at least one parent carries an *Ry* gene.
- Why it matters: with a single-copy parent only about half the seedlings inherit it, so a seedling marker screen has something to sort. That's the core of Aim 1.

### *S. microdontum* population: why greening and carotenoids, and in what tissue?
- **Greening** = chlorophyll forming under light in the tuber's **skin (periderm) and outer cortex**, in the field, in storage or on the shelf.
  - Harmless itself, but light also triggers glycoalkaloids (solanine, chaconine: bitter, toxic at high levels), and shoppers reject green tubers → culls and lost value, especially fresh-market.
  - Chlorophyll and glycoalkaloids are separate pathways with the same light trigger. Less greening ≠ fewer glycoalkaloids, which is likely why Prosser sent glycoalkaloid data for the mapping study.
- **Carotenoids** are mainly in the **tuber flesh**: yellow pigments (lutein, zeaxanthin, violaxanthin; little β-carotene). They set flesh color and market class, and add nutrition (antioxidants; lutein and zeaxanthin for eye health).
  - Caution: the classic yellow-flesh gene (*Y*, β-carotene hydroxylase) is on **chr 3**, but FY2025 reports a signal on **chr 5**. It may be a separate contributor from *S. microdontum*; don't call it *Y*.
- Why it matters to you: the Objective 2 case. Find the useful wild segment, then build markers to carry it into russets without the unwanted wild traits.
- The report doesn't name the tissue sampled; "outer tuber" and "tuber flesh" are the standard readings.

### Duplex × nulliplex → 5/6: what does it mean?
- Tetraploid = 4 copies per gene. With R = the *Ry* allele: nulliplex rrrr (0) · simplex Rrrr (1) · duplex RRrr (2) · triplex RRRr (3) · quadruplex RRRR (4).
- *Ry* is **dominant**: one copy = resistant. A seedling is susceptible only if rrrr.
- Each gamete gets 2 of 4 copies (6 ways):
  - Duplex RRrr → RR : Rr : rr = 1 : 4 : 1 (only 1/6 of gametes lack R).
  - Simplex Rrrr → Rr : rr = 1 : 1.
  - Nulliplex rrrr → all rr.
- Crossed with a **non-*Ry* (nulliplex)** parent:

| Cross | Susceptible | **Resistant (carry ≥ 1 R)** |
|---|---|---|
| Simplex × nulliplex | 1/2 | **1/2** |
| Duplex × nulliplex | 1/6 | **5/6** |
| Triplex/quadruplex × nulliplex | 0 | **all** |

- Other combinations, if asked: simplex × simplex = 3/4 resistant; duplex × simplex = 11/12.
- Double reduction slightly raises rr gametes from a duplex parent, so "~5/6" is an upper bound.
- Why it matters: dosage, not just presence, sets how many resistant seedlings a cross gives, and DArTag read counts can estimate parent dosage before crossing.
- The slide leaves out "crossed with a non-*Ry* parent"; say it out loud.

### Canopy traits from drone imagery, and the spectral bands needed

| Trait | Extract per plot | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Emergence | Date ~50% of plants up; uniformity | Seed vigor, stand, early frost exposure |
| 2. Canopy closure | Growth rate, date of max cover (logistic fit) | Light capture, early bulking |

- **Multispectral (N status, earlier senescence):** blue ~475, green ~560, red ~668, **red-edge ~717**, **NIR ~840 nm**. Use NDRE and red-edge chlorophyll indices, not NDVI (it saturates after closure). Calibrate every flight (reflectance panel + downwelling light sensor).
- **Thermal (8–14 µm), water stress only:** canopy temperature and the Crop Water Stress Index. Needs air temperature, humidity and wet/dry references. Easier on the dry Snake River Plain, but still the hardest to calibrate.
- **Flying:** weekly or better from emergence to closure and over the last 5–6 weeks before vine kill; within ±2 h of solar noon; fixed ground control points.

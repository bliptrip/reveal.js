This slide pays off the title, and it is meant to be spoken, not read. The title names an outcome (shortening the cycle) and instruments (phenotyping, mapping) but not the mechanism. Say the mechanism in these words: two cycles hide in the word "cycle". The recurrent breeding cycle (parent → parent) genuinely gets shorter with genomic prediction — you choose the next parents at seedling stage instead of waiting for pods. The cultivar release pipeline (cross → release, the program's ">14 years") mostly keeps its calendar years; what changes is time-to-decision. Royaert (MARS) knows the difference and will be listening for it.

  • The green loop: "Prediction models are trained on the genotyped, phenotyped trials and pay off on untested material — seedlings before planting and parents for the next cross. That is the recurrent cycle shortening. Prediction changes which and how many candidates enter a phase, not how many years the phase runs."

(4) Thermal calibration is the unglamorous cost: uncooled microbolometers drift with sensor body temperature — blackbody or in-scene references and a calibration campaign, budgeted alongside the sensors.

  • Honest about the imaging: cacao under shade trees is a harder problem than an open cranberry bed.   • Meinhardt bridge: SPCL is evaluating cacao genetic resources for drought tolerance and analysing core stress-response candidate genes — this aim is the population-scale, time-resolved phenotype that work lacks.
  • Why phenology is legitimately a Miami trait: the 2023 hardiness-zone update (11a) licensed the local populations; a cold event at 25°N is exactly the "when is the tissue exposed" question from the frost slide.
  • Genotypic variation in stomatal and photoprotective behaviour is documented (water-stress physiology in commercial clones, 2021; high-light response across accessions, 2021) — all hand-measured, one leaf at a time. That is the licence for thermal / spectral sensing.

Q&A only. Use if asked "which of these would you build for cacao?" — the two reference designs a cacao haplotype panel would sit between. Cacao is a single interfertile species with a ~430–445 Mb genome and usable variation that is entirely intraspecific, so the design question is the cranberry one (within-species density, uniform coverage, redesignable content), not the rhAmpSeq one (cross-genus transferability) — but the ascertainment lesson is rhAmpSeq's: design from all ten genetic groups, not from cultivated clones.

  • Same class of technology (targeted amplicon sequencing, reference-anchored panel, haplotype calls). The difference that matters is not chemistry but what fraction of the genome was targeted, and why: rhAmpSeq deliberately gave up ~91% of the genome to work in V. vinifera, riparia, rupestris, cinerea, muscadine and every hybrid between them; cranberry could afford whole-genome density because it is one species with a narrow gene pool — and paid for it with 45% missing SNP data in V. oxycoccos.
  • Nearly the same alleles per marker (5.7 vs 6.1) from a 27× difference in sampling (14 vs 384 chromosomes) — that is Vitis interspecific diversity vs cranberry's post-1800s bottleneck, not a platform difference.
  • Answer to "would you build it": no — rhAmpSeq exists, is validated at scale, and is already tiered into KASP. The open question is whether a higher-density, microhaplotype layer earns its per-sample cost for specific jobs: fine-mapping in a target population, or germplasm identity / clonal-purity audits. For cacao the answer flips: nothing of either kind exists, so the discovery tier is the proposal, with the 15K array's 1,000 anchor markers as the bridge to the ~1,300 samples already genotyped.
  • Terminology trap: if you say "core", specify which sense. rhAmpSeq's "collinear core genome" is about synteny across species; Flex-Seq's "core genes" is pangenome occupancy (Yocca et al. 2023). A gene can be one without the other.
  • Chemistry, one sentence if pushed: rhAmpSeq gates primer extension enzymatically (RNase H2); Flex-Seq uses two flanking probes; DArTag a single MIP-style oligo. Stop there.

Evaluating a trait: three questions, then an economic one

Repeatable?

Same material, different day, different scorer — characterise the instrument before the germplasm

cacao: no published inter-rater reliability or check-clone standard; Baek 2026 repeatability 0.78–0.89 on hand-scored trajectories

Heritable?

Genetic share of what remains after spatial and competition correction, on the unit I select on (clone vs parent)

cacao: disease h² 0.02–0.16 in progeny trials; 0.36–0.42 for FPR with spatial models (INIFAP 2024); yield components 0.37–0.64 (Royaert 2017)

Holds up?

Genetic correlation across the years and environments I deploy in — not a stability index

cacao: year-pair rg 0.41–0.94; 2 harvests → accuracy 0.85, 5 years → 0.93 (Rondônia clonal series, 2026); no multi-environment factor-analytic model across regions

ΔG = i · ρ · σA / L — a trait I can measure perfectly that doesn’t change a selection decision isn’t worth measuring
